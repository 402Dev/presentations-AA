What Speakers Have to Know

(Based on Martin Hilpert)

  • Know words and vocabulary.
  • Understand how to combine words into phrases and sentences.
  • Apply the correct endings to words (morphology).
  • Comprehend newly coined or unfamiliar words.
  • Recognize that intended meaning sometimes differs from the literal words said.
  • Adapt language use across different contexts and situations.
  • Know and understand idiomatic expressions.

Why not stick with dictionary-and-gramar model?

-> The view where we assume the knowledge of language is comprised of knowing words and the rules how to combine them into phrases fails when we consider idiomatic expressions such as “break a leg”, “as … as”, BIG MESS construction.

Enter Construction Grammar (CxG)

Construction Grammar argues that every observed linguistic utterance is an instantiation of one or more grammatical constructions.

A construction (cxn) refers to a form-meaning pair.

Lets define some terms

  • Form: The structural shape (words, grammar, sounds).
  • Meaning: The literal concept or information conveyed.
  • Function: The speaker's real-world intent or goal (e.g., a command, a joke).
  • Construct: The actual, physical utterance produced in the real world.

1. Morpheme Level

1
Morpheme

(The smallest unit)

Example: The plural marker -s

  • Form: The suffix -s (or -es).
  • Meaning: "More than one" or multiplicity.
  • Function: To quantify a noun for the listener.
  • Construct: The word “bugs” (in the utterance, "There are bugs in my code").

2. Word Level

2
Word

Example: The word Water

  • Form: The noun water (phonetically: /ˈwɔːtər/).
  • Meaning: The chemical substance H₂O.
  • Function: To identify or refer to the physical liquid in a discussion.
  • Construct: The spoken word “water” (in the utterance, "Pass me the water").

3. Fixed Idiom Level

3
Fixed Idiom

(Words locked together)

Example: Kick the bucket

  • Form: Verb (kick) + Definite Article (the) + Noun (bucket).
  • Meaning: To die. (Notice how the meaning of the whole is not the sum of its parts).
  • Function: To soften the blow of discussing death (euphemism) or add dark humor.
  • Construct: "My old laptop finally kicked the bucket yesterday."

4. Partially Filled Idiom

4
Partially Filled

(Some fixed words, some open slots)

Example: Drive [Someone] Crazy

  • Form: Verb (drive) + Object Slot (X) + Adjective (crazy).
  • Meaning: To cause someone to experience extreme annoyance or distress.
  • Function: Expressing frustration or dramatic hyperbole to get sympathy.
  • Construct: "This missing comma is driving me crazy."

5. Abstract Syntax Level

5
Abstract Syntax

(Pure structure, no fixed words)

Example: The Ditransitive Construction

  • Form: Subject + Verb + Noun Phrase 1 (Recipient) + Noun Phrase 2 (Theme).
  • Meaning: "X causes Y to receive Z" (Transfer of possession).
  • Function: To quickly highlight the recipient of an action without using prepositions (like "to" or "for").
  • Construct: "She handed him the dataset."

Form

The body of language
Form is every measurable trace a construction leaves behind.

In CxG, Form is not just surface grammar. It is a multi-dimensional fingerprint — slot order, syllable stress, morphological shape, and degree of abstraction — each tracked as its own structured field.

If Meaning is the mind, Form is the skeleton and the voice.
Why it matters for the database
Every formal dimension becomes a column.

On the Form side of CxG, the goal is to decompose the physical blueprint of a construction into discrete, queryable fields — topology, phonology, morphology, and schematicity.

Old assumption
Form = grammar rules in a textbook.

Syntax is an abstract system, entirely separate from meaning and the lexicon.

CxG shift
Form is a blueprint, unique to each construction.

The shape is a holistic specification — topology, phonology, morphology, and schematicity working together.

General CxG Organization
Separation of Concerns
Decoupling existence (Component Registry) from position (this matrix) avoids combinatorial explosion. No separate row is needed for every legal scrambled variation of a Farsi sentence.

Feature: Nesting & Recursion Profile

Syntactic Scope — The Network API Constraint

Schema location: Whole Construction. Governs how a construction may connect to the broader dependency network — whether it anchors the graph, embeds inside another node, or folds recursively into itself.

Database Data Types

is_root_only [Boolean]
If TRUE, this construction can only exist at the absolute top of the dependency graph. It cannot be a child node — e.g., English direct questions, Dutch V2 clauses.
embeddable_domains [Array<String>]
When is_root_only: FALSE, lists the slot types this construction is legally permitted to fill — e.g. [“Nominal_Slot”, “Adverbial_Slot”].
recursive_allowed [Boolean]
Can this construction embed instances of itself? Enables genitive chains, nested relative clauses, and other self-similar structures.
ROOT ONLY
Anchors the graph
Cannot be placed inside any other node. Attempting an illegal edge is a hard grammatical violation.
EMBEDDABLE
Fills a slot
Can occupy specific slot types in a parent construction as defined by embeddable_domains.
RECURSIVE
Embeds itself
The same construction template can appear inside one of its own slots — genitive chains, nested relative clauses, centre-embedding.

Nesting & Recursion — Utility

General CxG Organization
Graph Pruning
A language network is a massive multi-dimensional graph. By declaring is_root_only: TRUE, entire branches of the decision tree are pruned instantly. The parser mathematically knows not to attempt embedding root structures into subordinate slots.
Allo-constructions
A query for the “Interrogative” semantic family returns two competing forms: the Direct Question (is_root_only: TRUE) and the Indirect Question (embeddable: TRUE). The Nesting Profile algorithmically selects the correct form based on context.
LLM Error Diagnosis
Isolating Structural Violations
When a parser encounters a construction used in an illegal position, the Nesting Profile surfaces the exact failure: “A ROOT-ONLY construction was placed inside a Subordinate slot.” The error class is unambiguous — no feature guessing required.
Legal vs. Illegal Graph Edge
Subordinate slot ← ROOT ONLY ✕ Illegal edge
Subordinate slot ← EMBEDDABLE ✓ Legal edge

Feature: Morphosyntactic Flexibility Index

The “Frozenness” State — API Limits on Mutation

Schema location: Whole Construction. Defines which syntactic operations are mathematically permitted on this construction and which would destroy its meaning.

Database Data Types

is_fully_frozen [Boolean]
If TRUE, the construction is an absolute invariant string — no morphological changes permitted at all (e.g., “by and large”).
blocked_transformations [Array<Enum>]
When partially frozen, lists the specific operations that destroy meaning — e.g. [PASSIVIZATION, PLURALIZATION, CLEFTING].
allowed_transformations [Array<Enum>]
Operations that remain legally safe — e.g. [TENSE_SHIFT].
FULLY FROZEN
Absolute invariant
Zero mutations allowed. Any morphological change severs the idiomatic meaning entirely.
PARTIALLY FROZEN
Selective constraints
Specific operations blocked, others permitted. The most common state for idiomatic expressions.
FULLY FLEXIBLE
Open to all operations
Compositional constructions with no lexical anchors; any standard transformation applies.

Meaning

From Form to Scene
In CxG, meaning lives in patterns, not only in words.

Construction Grammar treats meaning as a cognitive scene: who is doing what to whom, with what result, stance, or social intent. The sentence shape contributes its own conceptual signal.

Meaning is more than a dictionary entry. It is a scene your mind can reconstruct.
Why it matters for the database
We stop tracking strings alone.

On the Meaning side of CxG, the goal is to map the abstract scene a speaker is trying to evoke, not just the surface words they typed.

Old assumption
Words contain meaning. Grammar just connects them.

In that model, syntax is mostly neutral plumbing.

CxG shift
The pattern itself carries meaning.

The construction adds its own conceptual payload beyond the dictionary.

Proof by nonsense sentence
You know more than the dictionary tells you.
“The engineer blicked the intern a rom.”

Even though blicked and rom are invented, most people still infer a transfer: the engineer caused the intern to receive something.

Feature: The Semantic Frame Anchor

The Scene & The Camera

Schema location: Whole Construction. The Anchor is the root conceptual node that names the real-world event and tells the parser where the speaker points the lens.

Database Data Types

frame_id [String / Foreign Key]
The universal cognitive scene being invoked: for example Commercial_Transaction, Actual_Transfer, or Physiological_State.
camera_focus [Array of Node IDs]
Which scene participants get pushed into grammatical spotlight, typically the Subject and Object positions.
backgrounded_elements [Array of Strings]
Entities that mathematically belong to the real-world scene, but are pushed out of frame and left unsaid.
The Anchor does two jobs at once: it labels the event and records the viewing angle from which the event is being narrated.
The Scene
What is actually happening in the world?

The frame is the abstract event model beneath the sentence: transfer, transaction, bodily state, social activity, and so on.

The Camera
Which parts of the scene get grammatical spotlight?

Two constructions can describe the same event while shifting attention toward different participants.

frame_id = EVENT
camera_focus = [Participants]
backgrounded = [Offscreen]
A dictionary tells you what a word can mean. The Semantic Frame Anchor tells you what event the construction is actually asking the system to log.

Feature: The Event Shape Profile

The Temporal Bounding Box & Playback Engine

UNBOUNDED

CONTINUOUS

ITERATIVE

HABITUAL

The Playback
How does time tick inside the event?

internal_rhythm specifies the temporal texture — a single flash, a smooth stream, a looping GIF, or a historical pattern.

Type-error checking

Feature: The Macro-Event Proposition

The Semantic Equation

Schema location: Whole Construction. Acts as the logical wrapper that consumes the individual Constituent Slots as its variables — a formalized equation encoding who does what to whom, and what follows.
